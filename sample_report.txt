Green Valley Community Library: Annual Report 2025

1. Introduction

The Green Valley Community Library was founded in 2011 by a group of local teachers who wanted every child in the neighborhood to have free access to books. In 2025 the library completed its fourteenth year of service. This report describes how the library used its funds, how many people it served, which programs were most successful, and what the board plans to do in 2026. The report is written for donors, volunteers, and members of the community council.

2. Membership and collection

By the end of 2025 the library had 1,240 registered members, an increase of 18 percent compared with 2024, when there were 1,051 members. About 60 percent of the members are children under the age of 16. The collection grew from 16,900 to 18,500 books during the year. Most of the new books were donated, but 2,300 books were bought with library funds. The most borrowed category was children's storybooks, followed by school textbooks and science magazines. The library also started a small collection of 120 audiobooks for visually impaired readers, which became popular within two months.

3. Finances

The approved budget for 2025 was 850,000 BDT. The library spent 790,000 BDT, which means 60,000 BDT was left unused and was moved to the reserve fund. Salaries for the three paid staff members took the largest share, 380,000 BDT. Books and magazines cost 210,000 BDT. Building repairs, electricity, and internet cost 150,000 BDT. The remaining 50,000 BDT was spent on events and printing. About 70 percent of the income came from donations by local businesses, 20 percent from membership fees, and 10 percent from a small government grant. The accounts were checked by an independent auditor in January 2026, and no problems were found.

4. The Khulna branch

The most important event of the year was the opening of a second branch in Khulna in March 2025. The branch started with 1,500 books that were moved from the main library and 900 new books. It is open six days a week and is run by a part-time librarian and eight volunteers. By December the Khulna branch had registered 310 members. The biggest challenge was the lack of reliable internet, which delayed the launch of the online borrowing system in that branch. The board decided to install a second internet connection in 2026 as a solution.

5. Programs and events

The library organized 48 events in 2025. The most successful was the Reading Month in October, which brought in 2,100 visitors over thirty days. During the Reading Month, children who read ten books received a certificate, and 214 children completed the challenge. Other popular programs were the weekly storytelling hour on Fridays, a coding club for teenagers that started in June with 22 students, and a monthly book swap. The library also ran two training sessions on how to use the internet safely, with 65 adult participants. Feedback forms showed that 92 percent of participants would recommend the library's events to a friend.

6. Volunteers

The library depends strongly on volunteers. In 2025 there were 36 active volunteers, who together worked more than 4,800 hours. The volunteers helped with shelving books, running events, repairing damaged books, and managing the Khulna branch. The board thanks the volunteers and plans to hold a training day for all volunteers in February 2026. Two long-serving volunteers, Mrs. Rahela Khatun and Mr. Anwar Hossain received special awards for more than ten years of service.

7. List of Donors and Supporters

In 2025, the Green Valley Community Library received support from a total of 14 donors. The largest contribution came from a local business, which donated 250,000 BDT. The remaining contributions were for smaller amounts. The library authorities extend their sincere thanks to all the donors. There are plans to organize an appreciation event for the donors in early 2026.

8. Plans for 2026

The board has approved a budget of 1,000,000 BDT for 2026. The primary objectives include creating a digital catalog to allow members to search for books online, installing a second internet connection at the Khulna branch, expanding the audiobook collection to 300 titles, and establishing a children’s reading corner in the main library. The board also aims to reach a membership of 1,600 by the end of the year. Progress updates will be provided to the community council every three months.
